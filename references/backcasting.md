## Purpose

Plan toward a desirable future by defining that future concretely first, then
reasoning backward through the milestones that must precede it, until the
chain connects to actions available today.

## Use When

- The goal is distant and forward planning keeps collapsing into extrapolating
  the present.
- Current constraints would veto every ambitious option if applied first.
- A team agrees on direction but not on the path or the next step.
- A long-term commitment needs to be translated into near-term, testable
  moves.

## Avoid When

- The environment is so volatile that any specific endpoint would be obsolete
  before the first milestone; use short adaptive loops instead.
- The desired future is contested among stakeholders and needs alignment
  before pathing.
- The endpoint is a vague aspiration that cannot be described in observable
  terms.
- The real problem is diagnosing the present, not reaching a future.

## Inputs

- A time horizon and a concrete description of the desired end state:
  observable conditions, magnitudes, and who benefits.
- Hard constraints that will still hold at the horizon.
- Today's verified starting position and available resources.
- Known irreversibilities, long lead times, and dependencies.

## Procedure

1. Describe the end state as if it already exists: what is observably true,
   for whom, at what scale. Check it against hard physical, legal, and
   resource constraints so the target is ambitious but not impossible.
2. Step backward from the end state: what must have been true immediately
   before it? Record each answer as a precondition.
3. Continue backward, precondition by precondition, until reaching conditions
   that already hold or actions startable now.
4. Where a precondition has several possible predecessors, branch and keep the
   alternatives visible instead of committing early.
5. Order the resulting chain forward and space milestones by lead time,
   putting long-lead and irreversible items earliest.
6. Mark the assumptions each link depends on, and define a leading indicator
   per milestone that shows the path is still viable.
7. Choose the first concrete moves and a review cadence at which the path,
   not just progress, is re-examined.
8. Revisit the end state itself when indicators show the path or the target
   has stopped being desirable or feasible.

## Guiding Questions

- What is observably true in the desired future, and for whom?
- What would have to be true the day before that future arrives?
- Which preconditions have the longest lead times or are irreversible?
- Where does the backward chain rely on a leap no one can explain?
- Which links depend on assumptions that events could falsify early?
- What is the first move that only makes sense on this path?
- What signal would tell us the path or the endpoint needs revision?

## Worked Example

A two-person newsletter wants a sustainable paid publication in three years:
5,000 paying subscribers and one full-time editor. Working backward: paid
conversion at that scale requires roughly 100,000 engaged free readers; that
requires an owned distribution channel rather than rented social reach; that
requires a repeatable format readers cite and share; that requires knowing
which of three current formats earns the most retention. The long-lead item is
building owned distribution, so it starts now. The first move is a two-month
format test with retention as the leading indicator, reviewed quarterly
against the backward chain rather than against raw subscriber counts.

## Common Pitfalls

- Describing the end state in aspirational language that cannot be observed
  or measured.
- Smuggling the present into the future by extrapolating current methods
  instead of asking what the end state itself requires.
- Building one brittle path and treating it as a forecast rather than one
  route among alternatives.
- Spacing milestones evenly over time instead of by lead time and
  irreversibility.
- Tracking progress against milestones while never re-examining whether the
  path or the endpoint still holds.
- Ignoring stakeholders who must sustain the end state after it is reached.

## Useful Combinations

- **First Principles** — verify the end state against fundamental constraints
  rather than convention.
- **Issue Trees** — decompose a milestone into the workstreams that produce
  it.
- **Inversion** — run a premortem on the path's weakest links.
- **OODA Loop** — execute near-term moves adaptively while the backward chain
  provides direction.
