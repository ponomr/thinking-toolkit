# Productive Thinking Model

**Category:** Problem solving
**Also known as:** Productive thinking process, Target Future process

## Purpose

Move from a defined problem through understanding, success criteria, generative
questions, divergent ideas, solution refinement, and an executable action plan.

## Use When

- A meaningful problem is defined but the solution is not.
- The team needs both creative divergence and disciplined convergence.
- Success criteria and execution resources must be connected.
- A complete workshop process is more useful than a single diagnostic tool.

## Avoid When

- The problem is routine and has a known reliable procedure.
- Immediate stabilization or safety action must happen first.
- The real issue is unresolved conflict, missing authority, or absent resources.
- The group cannot allocate time to a six-stage process.

## Inputs

- A bounded problem, affected stakeholders, and current evidence.
- A description of the desired future.
- Constraints, resources, values, and essential outcomes.
- Participants able to generate, evaluate, and own solutions.

## Procedure

1. **Ask "What is going on?"** Explore the situation, history, impact, evidence,
   stakeholders, and a Target Future in which the problem is resolved.
2. **Ask "What is success?"** Define success with DRIVE:
   - Do: what the solution must accomplish;
   - Restrictions: what it must not do or violate;
   - Investment: resources available;
   - Values: qualities the solution should embody;
   - Essential outcomes: results required for success.
3. **Ask "What is the question?"** Generate several catalytic questions, often
   beginning with "How might we," that connect the present to the Target Future.
4. **Generate answers.** Produce many possible answers without judging them.
   Build on, combine, and vary ideas.
5. **Forge the solution.** Evaluate candidates against the success criteria,
   combine strengths, address weaknesses, and select a coherent solution.
6. **Align resources.** Specify actions, owners, dependencies, resources,
   milestones, risks, and feedback.
7. Revisit earlier stages when later work exposes a weak frame or criterion.

## Guiding Questions

- What is happening, for whom, and with what impact?
- What evidence and prior attempts do we have?
- What does the Target Future look like in observable terms?
- What must the solution do, avoid, cost, embody, and achieve?
- Which question would unlock the largest useful solution space?
- Have we diverged enough before evaluating?
- What resources and ownership turn this into action?

## Output Format

- **What is going on:** problem map and Target Future.
- **What is success:** DRIVE criteria.
- **What is the question:** prioritized catalytic questions.
- **Generated answers:** unfiltered idea set and themes.
- **Forged solution:** selected concept and fit to criteria.
- **Aligned resources:** actions, owners, sequence, milestones, risks, review.

## Worked Example

A nonprofit wants more volunteers to complete onboarding. It maps where people
drop out, defines a Target Future of qualified volunteers starting within ten
days, and creates DRIVE criteria around safeguarding, staff capacity, clarity,
and completion. Catalytic questions produce ideas beyond shortening the form.
The team combines a guided group session with prefilled data and a clear status
tracker, then assigns owners and tests one cohort before a full rollout.

## Common Pitfalls

- Starting ideation before understanding the situation and success.
- Writing vague Target Futures that cannot guide evaluation.
- Treating DRIVE as a slogan rather than operational criteria.
- Converging after the first plausible answer.
- Selecting ideas through preference rather than success criteria.
- Ending with a concept but no resources, owner, or feedback loop.

## Useful Combinations

- **Abstraction Laddering** — improve the problem frame in the first stage.
- **Zwicky Box** — expand answer generation systematically.
- **Decision Matrix** — evaluate candidate solutions against DRIVE criteria.
- **OODA Loop** — execute and learn from the aligned plan iteratively.
