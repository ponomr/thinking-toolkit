# Cynefin Framework

**Category:** Decision making
**Also known as:** Sense-making domains

## Purpose

Classify a situation by the nature of cause and effect, then match the response
to that context instead of applying one management style everywhere.

## Use When

- It is unclear whether a problem needs procedure, expertise, experimentation,
  or immediate stabilization.
- Teams are arguing about certainty or the right type of evidence.
- A large situation contains parts with different levels of predictability.
- Applying familiar best practice to novelty may be dangerous.

## Avoid When

- The framework is being used as a permanent label for people or organizations.
- The issue is a narrow technical calculation with a known method.
- Classification would delay urgent stabilization.
- The user expects the domain label itself to produce a solution.

## Inputs

- A bounded situation or a set of decomposed sub-situations.
- What is known about causal relationships, stability, expertise, and feedback.
- Time pressure, constraints, and consequences of error.
- Evidence of novelty, emergence, conflict, or loss of control.

## Procedure

1. Bound the situation. Split it when different components behave differently.
2. Assess cause and effect, stability, and what can be known in time.
3. Place each component provisionally:
   - **Clear:** cause and effect are evident; use sense, categorize, respond and
     established practice.
   - **Complicated:** cause and effect are discoverable through analysis; use
     sense, analyze, respond and relevant expertise.
   - **Complex:** patterns emerge only through interaction; use probe, sense,
     respond through safe-to-learn experiments.
   - **Chaotic:** no usable constraint or stable relation is available; use act,
     sense, respond to establish stability first.
   - **Confusion:** the domain is unknown or contested; decompose and gather the
     minimum information needed to classify.
4. Select the corresponding action pattern and guardrails.
5. Watch for domain shifts as intervention and learning change the situation.
6. Record contradictory evidence and revisit classification.

## Guiding Questions

- Is the relationship between cause and effect evident, discoverable, emergent,
  or currently unusable?
- Is the situation stable and bounded?
- Would competent experts converge after analysis?
- Can small experiments reveal patterns safely?
- Must we first contain harm or establish order?
- Are different parts of the situation in different domains?
- What evidence would move this classification?

## Output Format

| Situation component | Domain | Evidence | Response pattern | Action | Review signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

Add the most dangerous misclassification and the guardrail that addresses it.

## Worked Example

A company faces declining customer retention. Password reset failures are Clear
and receive a standard repair procedure. A database performance issue is
Complicated and goes to specialists. A new community feature has uncertain
social dynamics, so it is Complex and receives several small, instrumented
experiments. A live outage is Chaotic and is contained before diagnosis. The
team avoids calling the entire retention problem "complex" and assigns a
different response to each component.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating Complex as merely very Complicated.
- Labeling an entire organization with one domain.
- Running uncontrolled experiments instead of bounded probes.
- Applying rigid best practice where context is changing.
- Staying in Chaotic mode after stability has been restored.
- Presenting a provisional classification as objective fact.

## Useful Combinations

- **OODA Loop** — run fast feedback cycles within a chosen response pattern.
- **Issue Trees** — decompose Confusion into classifiable components.
- **Productive Thinking Model** — develop solutions after the domain is clear.
- **Connection Circles** — investigate dynamics in a Complex situation.
