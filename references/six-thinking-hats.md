# Six Thinking Hats

**Category:** Decision making
**Also known as:** Parallel thinking

## Purpose

Separate different modes of thought so a person or group can examine one
question from multiple perspectives without turning every perspective into an
argument. Use the hats as temporary directions for attention, not as personality
types.

## Use When

- A decision, proposal, or problem is being discussed from only one angle.
- A group is stuck in advocacy, conflict, or repetitive debate.
- Creativity, evidence, emotion, opportunity, and risk all deserve attention.
- A facilitator needs a visible structure for a balanced discussion.

## Avoid When

- The task is a simple factual lookup or routine choice.
- The real need is quantitative comparison, causal diagnosis, or expert review.
- Participants cannot safely express concerns or emotions.
- The user needs one missing fact rather than more perspectives.

## Inputs

- A single, clearly worded focus question.
- The decision boundary and any non-negotiable constraints.
- Available facts, open questions, stakeholder reactions, and candidate ideas.
- A timebox and, for a group, a facilitator.

## Procedure

1. Use the Blue Hat to define the focus, desired outcome, sequence, and timebox.
2. Use the White Hat to list known facts, uncertain claims, and missing data.
3. Use the Red Hat to surface intuitions, emotions, and reactions without
   requiring justification.
4. Use the Yellow Hat to identify value, benefits, feasibility, and upside.
5. Use the Black Hat to identify risks, weaknesses, constraints, and failure
   conditions. Keep criticism evidence-based and constructive.
6. Use the Green Hat to generate alternatives, modifications, experiments, and
   ways around the Black Hat concerns.
7. Return to the Blue Hat to synthesize, decide what remains unresolved, and
   define the next action.
8. Change the sequence when the context requires it, but keep modes distinct.
   In groups, have everyone use the same hat at the same time to support parallel
   thinking.

## Guiding Questions

- **White:** What do we know? What is merely asserted? What must we learn?
- **Red:** What feels promising, worrying, or unacceptable to each stakeholder?
- **Yellow:** Where is the value? Under what conditions could this work well?
- **Black:** What could fail, violate a constraint, or create unacceptable harm?
- **Green:** What alternatives, combinations, or small tests have we not tried?
- **Blue:** What is the focus now? What have we learned? What happens next?

## Output Format

- **Focus question:** one sentence.
- **White Hat:** facts, unknowns, evidence needs.
- **Red Hat:** reactions and intuitions, attributed when appropriate.
- **Yellow Hat:** benefits and enabling conditions.
- **Black Hat:** risks and safeguards.
- **Green Hat:** alternatives and experiments.
- **Blue Hat synthesis:** decision, unresolved issues, next step, owner, review.

## Worked Example

A team is considering a four-day workweek. The White Hat separates measured
workload and service hours from assumptions about productivity. The Red Hat
reveals excitement about focus and anxiety about customer coverage. The Yellow
Hat identifies retention and recruitment benefits. The Black Hat highlights
handoff risk and peak-period capacity. The Green Hat proposes a six-week pilot
with staggered days off and explicit service metrics. The Blue Hat turns the
discussion into a reversible pilot rather than an immediate permanent policy.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assigning one permanent hat to each person and reinforcing stereotypes.
- Letting the Black Hat become unrestricted negativity or the Yellow Hat become
  unsupported optimism.
- Debating claims while a divergent hat is active instead of capturing them.
- Treating the six perspectives as evidence that a decision is correct.
- Ending without Blue Hat synthesis, ownership, or a review condition.

## Useful Combinations

- **Decision Matrix** — compare alternatives created by the Green Hat.
- **Inversion** — deepen the Black Hat into a disciplined premortem.
- **Ladder of Inference** — inspect disputed White Hat claims and assumptions.
- **Minto Pyramid** — communicate the Blue Hat synthesis clearly.
